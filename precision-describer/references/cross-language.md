# Cross-language precision

Use this reference only when a concept is genuinely expressed more precisely through another language, idiom, or untranslatable term.

## When to use

Surface a cross-language phrase only if it adds real semantic value that English alone would blur.

Good cases:
- a term names a culturally specific feeling or social pattern
- the foreign phrase is already common in the user's context
- the translated English alternatives are all noticeably flatter

Bad cases:
- it is used mainly for flair
- the audience is unlikely to understand it
- the nuance can be expressed just as clearly in English

## Output pattern

When using a cross-language term:
1. give the term
2. give a short gloss in English
3. explain the nuance that makes it more precise

Keep it brief. Do not turn the answer into a language lesson unless the user asks.

## Examples of the kind of move this enables

- surfacing a word that captures longing, social obligation, or aesthetic texture more cleanly than a long English paraphrase
- distinguishing literal meaning from lived connotation
- noting when a phrase is locally precise but globally opaque
