# Context calibration

Use this reference when precision depends on audience, domain, culture, or emotional register.

## Audience questions

Check:
- who will read this
- what background knowledge they likely have
- what they need to understand, feel, or decide
- what kind of wording they are likely to trust

Typical audience shifts:
- expert: tolerate denser terminology and sharper distinctions
- recruiter or evaluator: emphasize evidence, clarity, and relevance
- customer: emphasize benefits, usability, and concrete outcomes
- peer or community insider: allow idiom and shared shorthand
- student or learner: prefer explicit structure and unpacked terms

## Domain calibration

Different domains reward different kinds of precision:
- technical: mechanism, scope, constraints, measurable behavior
- literary: image, mood, cadence, connotation
- mathematical: definitions, distinctions, quantified relationships
- commercial: positioning, audience fit, value, differentiation
- colloquial: naturalness, recognizability, social register

Do not import domain vocabulary just to sound smart. Use it only when it reduces ambiguity for the target audience.

## Cultural and community context

Check whether the wording depends on:
- regional idiom
- online community norms
- subcultural terms of art
- connotation that may shift across groups

If a phrase is precise only inside a narrow community, surface that limitation.

## Emotional register

Tune for feeling as well as denotation:
- neutral: direct, restrained, low-color wording
- warm: human, encouraging, relational
- intense: vivid, forceful, compressed
- formal: controlled, explicit, low slang
- playful: surprising, light, idiomatic

When choosing between equally precise terms, prefer the one that fits the intended emotional register.
